# Thesis

**Pitch.** Meera presents a user-ready demurrage claim before day 90: SERV Multipath rules every hour of the ship's wait against three rulebooks that contradict each other, code keeps the clock, and her own wallet signs the presentation.

**Twenty-second hook.** A shipowner lost a demurrage claim in the English High Court because the bills of lading were not attached by day 90. Laydays rules every hour of the wait against three rulebooks, checks the document list the charter party demands, and a contract on Base refuses the claim if it is late or incomplete.

**X without Y.** Get paid for the wait without losing the claim to the calendar.

**Primary guarantee.** A claim Laydays lets Meera present is on time, carries every document her charter party demands, and every hour in it points to the clause and the log line that decided it.

**The loop.** Log in, rulings, review, signature, ledger, funding, answer, settlement. Both people are in it.

**The crew.** Port Clerk, Clause Reader, Senior Reader, Time-bar Clerk, Counter Reader, Statement Writer, and three pieces of plain code: Citation Checker, Laytime Clock, time bar watch. One responsibility each, typed outputs.

**Why the name.** Laydays are the days a charterer is allowed for loading or discharging. The product is about exactly those days.

**What this is not.** It is not a guard for AI agents, not a policy layer and not a dashboard. The headline is Meera's claim.
