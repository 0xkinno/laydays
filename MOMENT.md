# The person, the moment, the cost

## Persona (fictional, labelled)

- **Meera Pillai**, demurrage analyst at **Saltmarsh Tankers**, a nine-ship chemical tanker operator in Singapore.
- **Dan Reyes**, claims manager at **Gulfline Trading**, the charterer, in Houston.
- Vessel: **MT Orla Jansen**, 19,900 dwt. Voyage: Antwerp to Houston. Completion of discharge 22 August 2026, 14:10 local. Time bar: 90 days.

All of them are fictional. The job is real.

## The moment

Day 83 of 90, 17:40 in Singapore. Meera has eleven open claims. The *Orla Jansen* waited three days and nine hours beyond its laytime at Houston (the figure a spreadsheet gets by counting every hour is $61,250.00 at $18,000 a day). The time bar clause lists seven documents. One, the bills of lading, is in an email thread.

## What she does today

1. Types 41 log lines into a spreadsheet.
2. Reads the charter party clause by clause and decides, hour by hour, what counts.
3. Builds a laytime statement in a template.
4. Gathers the seven documents by email.
5. Emails the claim and hopes it arrived complete and in time.
6. Waits weeks for the charterer, who disputes one item and holds the whole payment.

## Everyone assumes X; in reality Y

Everyone assumes the hard part of a demurrage claim is the arithmetic. In reality the arithmetic is the easy part. The money is lost in the reading, which hours a clause excepts, and in the calendar, a complete claim presented before the bar.
