# Laytime definitions, in our own words

Source of the ideas: the BIMCO Laytime Definitions for Charter Parties (2013), published by BIMCO, and settled English case law on laytime. These are our own plain-language summaries, not a copy of any text.

L-1 Laytime is the time the charterer is allowed for loading or discharging. It runs continuously unless the charter party stops it.
L-2 A notice of readiness is valid only when the vessel has arrived where the charter party says it may tender notice and is ready in all respects. Missing free pratique, when the charter party requires it, makes the notice invalid.
L-3 Time that is not excepted counts, including time spent waiting for a berth when the charter party says "whether in berth or not".
L-4 An exception stops the clock only for the cause it names. Delay on the shore side that no clause names counts against the charterer.
L-5 Once the vessel is on demurrage, exceptions do not stop the clock unless the clause says it applies on demurrage.
L-6 Shifting between berths counts as laytime or time on demurrage unless the charter party gives it another rate.
L-7 A port closure caused by weather is not a weather exception under the charter party unless the charter party says so.
L-8 Time between recorded events is judged by the state that began at the earlier event and lasted until the next one.
